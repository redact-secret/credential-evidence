## Vocabulary

- **ready**: the contract names a carrier (a header, a field, a response member). A positive Case is authored for it, with the exact span, and a control Case for placeholders and non-values.
- **carrier-unresolved**: the contract establishes a role or an existence, and no carrier (or a carrier shared with another role that the evidence cannot separate). The record is `not-assertable`.
- **policy-limited**: a carrier may be named, but whether the value is confidential, public or a finding is not settled by the evidence (frontend-intended, unstated, or the project's exposure policy). The record is `not-assertable`. `may-flag` is not used: no contract states a tolerance, and the corpus snapshot cannot carry it (ADR 0021, ADR 0023).

## What was not asserted, and why

- **Algolia roles other than admin on the create-key request.** Every role travels in `x-algolia-api-key` and the search-only key is documented as frontend-safe, so a header-only positive would contradict the contract. The role is attributable from a variable name, a description or an ACL only, none documented as a marker. The one admin positive rests on the documented endpoint requirement; its path in the fixtures is a synthetic stand-in, not a documented path.
- **Application IDs, Instagram App ID, space and environment IDs, Figma plan token id, JFrog token id, Dropbox app key.** No page read calls them non-secret, so none is a literal in a `must-not-flag` fixture. The unresolved benign siblings stay unresolved.
- **Derived values.** The secured key, the JFrog master token and the Canva `code_challenge` have no confidentiality statement, so no derived-value control was written for them; the Zoom derived outputs and the Meta `appsecret_proof` already have controls (see below).
- **`may-flag`.** Not used for any row (see the vocabulary).
